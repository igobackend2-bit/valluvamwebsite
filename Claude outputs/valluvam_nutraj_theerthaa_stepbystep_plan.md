# Making Valluvam Look More Professional — Step-by-Step Plan
### Based on a direct analysis of Nutraj.com and Theerthaa.com

Theerthaa is the closer match — it sells almost the exact same mix as Valluvam (cold-pressed oils, ghee, millets, rice, spices, dry fruits) — so most of the strongest, most directly-copyable ideas below come from there. Nutraj is a bigger, more corporate dry-fruits brand and contributes the loyalty/rewards and content-marketing ideas. This is a plan only — nothing has been changed on valluvamproducts.com.

---

## What I found on each site

**Nutraj.com**
- A scrolling row of product icons (Pista, Raisin, Walnut, Fig, Dates, Almond, Cashew) right under the header — instant visual browsing before a shopper even scrolls.
- Every product card shows: brand name, MRP struck through, sale price, and critically, a **price-per-unit** ("₹475 / 100g") — makes comparing pack sizes effortless.
- Out-of-stock items show a clear "Sold out" badge with a **"Notify Me"** button instead of a dead or missing Add to Cart.
- A trust strip with 4 icons: Free Shipping above ₹1499, Pay on Delivery, 100% Quality Guaranteed, **Reward Points on Every Purchase**.
- A "Shop By Purpose" section — Snacking, Daily Nutrition, Gifting, Cooking — lets shoppers browse by *why* they're buying, not just by product type.
- Real stats bar: "11+ Stores, 164,250+ Happy Customers, 182,500+ Orders Delivered" plus a physical store locator.
- Testimonials that mention specific products by name ("Anmol Walnuts," "their walnuts are super crunchy") instead of generic praise.
- A blog with real, dated, festival-relevant posts (Rakhi gifting, seasonal health content) — active content marketing.
- A genuinely useful FAQ section (10 real health/usage questions like "which dry fruit is best for weight loss," not just shipping/returns).

**Theerthaa.com**
- A "Shop By Categories" icon grid at the very top with **every category clearly labeled in text** (Cold Pressed Oils, Pure Cow Ghee, Millets & Health Mix, Curry Powder & Spices, Traditional Rice & Poha, etc.) — this is the exact fix Valluvam's homepage category grid needs, since Valluvam's cards currently all just say "Explore Category" with no name.
- **Every product card has its own size selector right in the grid** (200mL / 500mL / 1L / 5L / 16.5L) — a shopper can pick a size and add to cart without ever opening the product page. This is a step beyond what Valluvam has (Valluvam's size selector only appears after opening a product page).
- Products are tagged with lifestyle/health-goal chips (Fitness Fuel, Kids, Couple, Office Desk, Mother's Care, Gen Silver) and there's a dedicated **"Choose By Health Goals"** section — the same self-segmentation idea as Nutraj's "Shop By Purpose," executed with clearer visual tags.
- Consistent price formatting everywhere: sale price, MRP struck through, %OFF badge, on every single card.
- Clear "SOLD OUT" badges on out-of-stock items.
- An "About Us" block with specific, real-sounding numbers ("200+ Products, 20+ Farmer Partnerships") rather than vague claims.
- Reviews explicitly sourced from Google ("4.9★ rated in Google Reviews") with named customers who mention specific products (ghee, groundnut oil) — reads as far more credible than a handful of unattributed quotes.
- An active "Express your Reviews" call-to-action, meaning they're still collecting new reviews, not just displaying old ones.
- A working WhatsApp click-to-chat link (Valluvam already has this — no gap here).

## Step-by-step plan for Valluvam

### Step 1 — Fix the category grid to match Theerthaa's clarity
Right now Valluvam's 14 homepage category cards all read "Explore Category" with no visible name. Add the category name as visible text on every card (Rice, Spices, Cold-Pressed Oils, Millets, Nuts, Dry Fruits, Combos, etc.), the same way Theerthaa labels every one of its 12 categories clearly. This is the single highest-impact, lowest-effort fix on the list.

### Step 2 — Add price-per-unit to every product card
Copy Nutraj's pattern: show "₹XXX / 100g" or "₹XXX / kg" next to the price on every product card and product page, not just the total pack price. For a site selling the same items in 1kg through 25kg packs, this is the single easiest way to help a shopper compare value across sizes at a glance.

### Step 3 — Bring the weight/size selector onto the product grid, not just the product page
Valluvam's size selector already works well on the product detail page. Theerthaa goes one step further and puts the size selector directly on the homepage/category grid card, so a shopper can pick "1L" or "5L" and add to cart without opening the product. Bringing this forward to the grid would put Valluvam ahead of where it is now and match the most effective pattern seen on either site.

### Step 4 — Fix how out-of-stock items are shown
Both competitor sites handle this the same way: a clear "SOLD OUT" badge replacing the Add to Cart button, and on Nutraj, a "Notify Me" button in its place. Check whether Valluvam currently has any out-of-stock handling at all — if not, this is a real gap versus both sites.

### Step 5 — Add a visible loyalty/rewards signal
Nutraj puts "Reward Points on Every Purchase" in its main 4-icon trust strip, right alongside free shipping and quality guarantee — it's treated as equally important as those. Valluvam doesn't have a loyalty program yet (flagged in the earlier plan); even before building the full mechanics, this is worth prioritizing given how prominently a direct competitor features it.

### Step 6 — Add a "shop by purpose / health goal" section
Both sites independently converged on the same idea: let shoppers browse by *why* they're buying (Nutraj: Snacking, Daily Nutrition, Gifting, Cooking; Theerthaa: Kids, Fitness Fuel, Office Desk, Mother's Care, Couple, Gen Silver) rather than only by product category. For Valluvam this could look like: "Everyday Cooking," "Wholesale/Business," "Gifting," "Traditional Cooking (rice & millets)," "Healthy Snacking (nuts & dry fruits)."

### Step 7 — Make testimonials specific and sourced
Valluvam's four homepage testimonials are short and generic. Both competitors show testimonials that name specific products (Theerthaa: "especially the ghee," "their groundnut oil, eggs are some of my favourite"; Nutraj: "especially walnuts"). Rewrite or collect new testimonials that mention actual Valluvam products by name, and where possible, label them as sourced from Google Reviews or a verified order, the way Theerthaa does ("4.9★ rated in Google Reviews") — this directly addresses the "are these real customers" credibility gap flagged earlier.

### Step 8 — Add real, specific brand-story numbers
Instead of only qualitative claims, add a small stats block with real numbers the way both sites do (Nutraj: stores/customers/orders; Theerthaa: "200+ Products, 20+ Farmer Partnerships"). For Valluvam this could be something like years in business, number of SKUs, number of wholesale partners, or cities delivered to — whatever is actually true and can be stated honestly.

### Step 9 — Start light content marketing
Nutraj runs a blog with dated, festival-tied posts (Rakhi gift guides, health-benefit articles) that double as SEO content and repeat-visit reasons. Valluvam's site already has a "Blog" link in its footer navigation — worth checking whether it's active, and if not, this is a good candidate for simple, low-cost content (festival gifting guides for dry fruits/oils, "how to store rice," "cold-pressed vs refined oil" explainers).

### Step 10 — Expand the FAQ beyond shipping/returns
Valluvam's current FAQ covers wholesale, delivery, payment, and returns — all operational. Nutraj's FAQ also answers genuine product questions ("which dry fruit is best for weight loss," "how to store dry fruits"). Adding a handful of category-specific questions (e.g., "how to store cold-pressed oil," "which rice is best for idli," "how long do nuts stay fresh") would both help SEO and reduce pre-purchase hesitation.

---

## Suggested order

1. Step 1 (category labels) and Step 4 (stock badges) — these are quick, visible fixes.
2. Step 2 (price-per-unit) and Step 7 (specific testimonials) — low effort, meaningfully raises perceived trust and usability.
3. Step 3 (grid-level size selector) and Step 6 (shop-by-purpose section) — slightly more UI work, but directly copy a pattern proven on a near-identical competitor.
4. Step 5 (loyalty signal) and Step 8 (real stats block) — need real numbers/program design decided first.
5. Step 9 (blog) and Step 10 (expanded FAQ) — ongoing content work, can start anytime in parallel.
